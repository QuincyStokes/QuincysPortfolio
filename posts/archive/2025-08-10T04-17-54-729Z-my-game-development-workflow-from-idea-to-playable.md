# My Game Development Workflow: From Idea to Playable

Today I want to share my approach to game development - the process I've developed over the years that helps me turn ideas into actual playable experiences.

## The Idea Phase

Every game starts with an idea, but not every idea becomes a game. Here's how I filter and develop concepts:

### 1. **The Spark**
- Usually comes from playing other games
- Sometimes from real-life experiences
- Occasionally from dreams or random thoughts
- Often from combining two existing concepts in a new way

### 2. **The Filter Questions**
Before committing to an idea, I ask myself:
- **Is this fun to think about?** If I'm not excited about the concept, players won't be either
- **Can I actually make this?** Be realistic about scope and skills
- **Is there something unique here?** Even if it's a familiar genre, what's my twist?
- **Will I still care about this in 6 months?** Long-term motivation is crucial

## Planning and Design

### **Paper First, Code Later**
I always start with paper and pencil:
- Sketch out the core mechanics
- Draw UI mockups
- Write down the player's journey
- Map out progression systems

### **The One-Page Design Document**
I force myself to fit the entire game concept on one page:
- Core gameplay loop
- Key mechanics (max 3-5)
- Target experience
- Success metrics

## Development Approach

### **Prototype Fast**
- Get something playable in the first week
- Don't worry about art or polish initially
- Focus on the core "fun factor"
- Test with friends and family immediately

### **Iterate Relentlessly**
My typical cycle:
1. Build a feature
2. Playtest it
3. Identify what's not fun
4. Fix or remove it
5. Repeat

### **The 80/20 Rule**
- 80% of the fun comes from 20% of the features
- Identify and polish those core features
- Cut everything else ruthlessly
- It's better to have 3 polished mechanics than 10 mediocre ones

## My Current Workflow Tools

### **Unity + C#**
- **Why Unity?** Rapid prototyping, huge community, great documentation
- **C# Benefits:** Strong typing, great IDE support, extensive libraries
- **Workflow:** ScriptableObjects for data, Events for communication, Coroutines for timing

### **Version Control**
- **Git** for code
- **Git LFS** for large assets
- **Feature branches** for each major system
- **Regular commits** (at least daily)

### **Project Management**
- **Trello** for task tracking
- **Notion** for design documents
- **Daily standups** with myself (seriously!)
- **Weekly reviews** of progress and goals

## The Playtesting Cycle

### **Internal Testing (Daily)**
- Play my own game every day
- Take notes on what feels off
- Record gameplay sessions
- Look for "fun moments" and "frustration points"

### **Friend Testing (Weekly)**
- Get fresh eyes on the project
- Watch people play without explaining
- Ask specific questions about their experience
- Don't defend your design - just listen

### **Public Testing (Monthly)**
- Release alpha builds
- Gather feedback from the community
- Look for patterns in feedback
- Prioritize fixes based on impact

## Common Pitfalls I've Learned to Avoid

### **1. Feature Creep**
- Every new idea seems like it will make the game better
- Reality: Most features make the game more complex, not more fun
- Solution: Write down ideas for "version 2" and focus on the core

### **2. Perfectionism**
- Spending too much time on one feature
- Reality: Good enough is often better than perfect
- Solution: Set time limits and move on

### **3. Ignoring Player Feedback**
- Thinking I know what players want better than they do
- Reality: Players are great at identifying problems, not always at solutions
- Solution: Listen to the problem, design the solution myself

## My Success Metrics

I measure success by:
- **Time to fun:** How long until a new player experiences the core fun?
- **Retention:** Do people want to play again?
- **Word of mouth:** Do people recommend it to friends?
- **Personal satisfaction:** Am I proud of what I created?

## What's Working Right Now

### **GEODE Development**
- Daily playtesting sessions
- Weekly feature sprints
- Monthly community feedback
- Clear roadmap with realistic milestones

### **Starlight Stray Post-Mortem**
- Documenting what worked and what didn't
- Planning improvements for future projects
- Analyzing player feedback from the game jam

## Tips for Fellow Developers

1. **Start small** - Your first game should be tiny
2. **Finish something** - A complete small game is better than an incomplete big one
3. **Play your own game** - Every single day
4. **Get feedback early** - Don't wait until you think it's ready
5. **Document everything** - Future you will thank present you
